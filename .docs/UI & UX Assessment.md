# UI & UX Assessment — Landing Page Baitani

> Review basis: running development server on branch `fitur-kamu`, opened in Chrome on 11 October 2026. Desktop screenshots and page structure were reviewed for the homepage; mobile viewport emulation at 390×844 was used for the homepage and the Acara, Lokasi, Persembahan, Koneksi, Connect Group, and Sorotan routes. Tentang Kami and Gembala were visually inspected at desktop size. Source was checked against observations and read-only findings from three native subagents (journey map, interaction/accessibility review, content-trust audit). This is a qualitative assessment, not a full WCAG audit, user study, or a runtime check of every control and route. No application files were changed.
>
> **Design read:** church landing site for first-time visitors, members, and people seeking services, using a cinematic editorial direction with a dark sacred-gold visual language. Taste-skill baseline for this landing-page reading: variance 8, motion 6, density 4. These dials describe the intended visual character, not a measured quality score.

## Detailed observations

At desktop scale, the site makes a decisive first impression: an almost-black canvas, large white uppercase display typography, muted gold accents, and full-bleed worship photography make the brand feel dramatic, premium, and recognizably consistent. The `WELCOME HOME` carousel occupies nearly all of the first screen, with the logo and a single-line primary navigation at the top, short gold eyebrow above the headline, Indonesian welcome copy, two calls to action, and numbered slide controls along the bottom. The generous negative space gives the headline authority and avoids the clutter of a conventional church information portal. The trade-off is that the visual brand moment dominates over immediate practical information: a new visitor must decide whether to use the gold-and-white calls to action or explore eight small, tightly packed top-level links before seeing service times, a location, or a person to contact. The first hero CTA promises service location and schedule, which makes its destination defect especially damaging to the core first-visit task.

The visual system is coherent across the sampled pages rather than a collection of unrelated templates. Deep obsidian, charcoal surfaces, white text, and warm gold are reflected in the CSS tokens (`src/app/globals.css:3-27`); the Cinzel display face and Plus Jakarta Sans body face support a clear difference between ceremonial headlines and practical reading. Gold rules, eyebrow labels, breadcrumb text, outlined pills, and rounded buttons repeat throughout the routes. The homepage and route hero images give the dark surfaces depth, and alternating surface tones separate sections without changing the identity. The visual language is intentionally high-contrast and theatrical; however, repeated all-caps, wide-tracked micro-labels and gold italic English accents make many pages share the same cadence. They preserve brand continuity but flatten the distinction between an editorial story, a practical schedule, and a form task. On the About and Gembala samples, large quiet spans around the header/profile sections create a gallery-like pace; that suits a brand story but delays task information on a phone.

The homepage composition is now deliberately compact: the runtime showed a full-screen carousel, an animated annual-theme ticker, an events strip, a highlights strip, and then the footer. This gives a quick promotional overview and offers clear onward links such as “Semua Acara” and “Semua Galeri.” The full-page desktop capture also shows the limitation of the composition: after a very tall hero, the visitor reaches three event cards and a horizontal photo strip, then a substantial footer; the church’s identity, location, pastoral profile, giving information, and newcomer connection form are not embedded on the landing page. Those subjects have dedicated routes, so the site can still be navigated, but the homepage no longer tells a complete scroll-based story. This conflicts with the previously documented hybrid-homepage goal and changes the visitor journey from “discover the church in sections” to “choose a page from navigation.” Treat that as a product/IA decision, not merely a visual adjustment.

The hero is visually strong but its interaction contract is unreliable. The four slides rotate automatically on a 6.5-second timer, support previous/next controls on larger screens, direct selection through a two-row timeline on small screens, keyboard arrow handling, and touch-swipe handling (`src/components/sections/HeroSection.tsx:7-61,161-240`). At a 390×844 viewport the title wraps cleanly into two lines, both hero calls to action remain full-width and readable, and the timeline becomes a compact two-column control. The homepage’s primary call to action was clicked in Chrome: the URL became `/#lokasi`, but the page had no `id="lokasi"` target and remained at scroll position zero. The other fragment-based CTAs, including the ones in the homepage event cards, point to sections/forms no longer mounted there (`src/data/heroSlidesData.ts:8-65`; `src/components/sections/EventsCarousel.tsx:115-121`). The visible CTA therefore promises a destination that the user does not reach. This is the clearest immediate conversion-path failure. The hero’s play/pause control is hidden below the `sm` breakpoint (`HeroSection.tsx:230-240`), leaving mobile users without the visible pause affordance available on desktop while slides continue changing. The code also does not connect autoplay to the reduced-motion preference, even though the global CSS reduces animation duration; the slide content itself can still change on a timer.

Mobile layout is generally contained and touch-first in the samples. At 390px, the homepage document did not exceed viewport width; event and gallery rails use internal horizontal scrolling and snap, with a partial next card indicating that more content exists. The Acara page’s category filters wrap into several tidy rows above a full-width search field; event imagery and text are stacked in a legible single column. Lokasi exposes campus selection as large pill buttons, while Persembahan presents account information in a single column, and Koneksi/Connect Group use vertically stacked sections and form content. This avoids desktop columns merely shrinking until unreadable. The deliberate drawback is repeated long vertical scrolling: route headers, explanatory copy, large section gaps, and stacked cards push practical choices down. The homepage’s horizontal rails conceal their scrollbars (`.no-scrollbar` in `src/app/globals.css:136-143`), so the next-card peek and arrow buttons carry the discoverability burden. Actual touch hit-target dimensions, small-device breakpoints below 390px, and landscape behavior were not exhaustively tested.

Navigation communicates the site map reasonably well on desktop: the shared header exposes eight destinations, highlights the current route by gold text/underline, and keeps a newcomer CTA visible (`src/components/common/Navbar.tsx:20-29,97-138`). Breadcrumbs on dedicated route heroes give users a clear route back to Beranda, and the footer repeats a complete list of routes. On mobile the desktop links collapse to a hamburger; the visible control has an accessible name, but the source does not set `aria-expanded` or `aria-controls`, and the menu container is not labelled by an id (`Navbar.tsx:140-173`). Escape-key dismissal and explicit focus treatment are also not apparent in the source. The “menu” looks like a disclosure, not a modal, so a focus trap is not automatically appropriate; the important questions are whether opening state is announced, whether focus remains visually apparent, whether closing is predictable, and whether the drawer’s position/contrast remains usable against the page. I could not reliably verify the drawer’s open-state interaction in this browser session, so its runtime behavior remains an explicit gap rather than a confirmed visual defect. The hero slide selectors likewise use `focus:outline-none` without an obvious replacement focus ring (`HeroSection.tsx:189-195`), while the global tokens do not define a shared focus-visible treatment. Keyboard appearance should be checked directly before making a conformance claim.

The most serious experience risk is trustworthiness of the content, not the typography. The giving page visually labels accounts “Rekening Resmi Gereja Baitani” and “TERVERIFIKASI,” yet the source hardcodes obvious sequential account values such as `1234567890`, `9876543210123`, and `556677889900` (`src/app/persembahan/page.tsx:12-33,88-123`). In the mobile screenshot the first account number and the “TERVERIFIKASI” badge were visible together. The presence of copy-account controls makes these values actionable; if the values are not officially supplied and verified, this design invites a real financial mistake. The annual homepage event rail advertises October–December 2026 activities, while `/acara` lists May–August 2026 items that are already past as of the review date and visually marks registrations as open or seats remaining (`src/data/eventsData.ts`; `src/app/acara/page.tsx:29-113,270-298`). The two feeds also use separate data sets. These contradictions make the calendar feel fictional or unattended. Replace or clearly mark draft content and do not use authenticity badges until the church verifies each value.

Other trust cues need the same content-owner review. Footer and newcomer pages expose a plausible address, telephone/WhatsApp number, and email as if they were live church contact details (`src/components/common/Footer.tsx:186-216`; `src/app/koneksi/page.tsx`); social icons currently point to generic platform homepages rather than Baitani profiles (`Footer.tsx:62-95`). Hero, event, and gallery photography is predominantly remote Unsplash stock; an inline comment explicitly says hero assets are replaceable (`src/data/heroSlidesData.ts:3-6`). A local portrait is now shown for the lead pastor on `/gembala`, but the surrounding pages continue to use generic stock imagery. Further, the route-level pastoral list and shared leadership data do not fully agree: both include Pdt. Johanes Pratama, but the source names/roles vary for the other team members (compare `src/app/gembala/page.tsx:16-57` and `src/data/leadershipData.ts:3-33`). The gallery’s testimonials are presented as named member quotations (`src/app/sorotan/page.tsx`); source review alone cannot establish that they are real. These details affect whether a first-time visitor sees an authentic local community or an attractive but unverified mock-up.

The forms and registration controls have polished affordances, but currently imply more function than the application provides. `/acara` changes a card to “Pendaftaran Berhasil!” for three seconds after a local state update; it does not send a registration to a service (`src/app/acara/page.tsx:24-27,134-139,280-298`). ConnectIntentForm and Connect Group similarly render local success state without a submission endpoint (see `src/components/sections/ConnectIntentForm.tsx` and `src/app/connect-group/page.tsx`). On the homepage, the event cards’ “Daftar” links instead target the missing `#koneksi` fragment. Thus the same apparent action has two different outcomes: a dead anchor from the homepage and a transient success message from the detail route, neither of which confirms actual registration. On the newcomer page, multiple intent buttons lead into a detailed form and the interface states that information is confidential; those promises become materially sensitive if users enter phone numbers or prayer/counseling information, so an apparent submission success without an actual recipient or explicit demo disclosure is not safe to present as a live service. Error, pending, confirmation, privacy, and delivery behavior need product/backend grounding.

Overall, the site already has a memorable, internally consistent visual identity and a strong mobile-first stacking pattern. Its principal weakness is the gap between the confidence of its visual promises and the reliability of the destinations/content behind them: dead homepage anchors, past and conflicting event schedules, mock giving details labelled verified, and forms that simulate completion. The best improvement order is therefore trust and task completion before decorative polish: (1) verify/remove financial and contact claims and remove unsupported “verified” language; (2) reconcile event dates and datasets and decide whether these are real listings or sample content; (3) make every visible CTA reach a truthful, existing destination and never indicate successful registration unless it is actually recorded; (4) settle the homepage information-architecture contract; (5) audit mobile menu, keyboard focus, reduced-motion/autoplay, form errors/privacy, and scroll-rail accessibility; then (6) refine repeated micro-labels, below-the-fold pacing, real Baitani photography, and social profile links. These are review priorities, not edits made in this session.

## Evidence and limits

- **Direct browser observations:** desktop homepage first screen and full-page composition; desktop About and Gembala first screens; mobile 390×844 first screen of homepage, Acara, Lokasi, Persembahan, Koneksi, Connect Group, and Sorotan. Homepage hero CTA clicked; observed `/#lokasi`, `document.getElementById('lokasi') === null`, and `scrollY === 0`. Homepage document width was 390px; the event and highlights strips had internal scroll widths of 1,224px and 1,444px within 358px containers.
- **Source-grounded review:** routes, anchor ids/URLs, event data, bank-account labels, local-only form state, palette and responsive classes. Content accuracy is not confirmed by code; claims needing church signoff remain unverified.
- **Subagent corroboration:** three native read-only passes covered visitor journeys, interaction/accessibility, and content authenticity. Their conclusions were treated as leads and checked against source; not every categorical rating was accepted. In particular, a navigation disclosure should not be labelled WCAG-failing merely because it does not trap focus, and this review does not claim a formal WCAG audit.
- **Not tested:** full route-by-route desktop and keyboard walkthrough, real-device Safari/Android, measured color contrast, screen-reader behavior, reduced-motion behavior in-browser, back-end delivery, data-owner authenticity, or formal accessibility conformance.
